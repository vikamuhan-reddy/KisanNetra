A field sentinel that knows when it isn't sure.

KisanNetra is a fixed, solar-powered pole for a paddy field in the Cauvery delta. It photographs the crop, reads the soil and air, and decides on the device, with no network whether there is disease, pest damage, a nutrient problem or a need to irrigate. When the evidence is weak, it says so and looks again next cycle instead of guessing.

The problem

Crop decisions are made late, far from the field, on a connection that isn't there

The problem statement asks for a field-deployable, AI-powered assistant that detects disease, pests, nutrient deficiency and irrigation need early, running as edge AI on the device and working without continuous cloud access.

  • Where: Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu's rice bowl. Smallholder plots with unreliable connectivity.
  • Who: a farmer who will not stand in flooded paddy photographing leaves with a phone.
  • Constraint: any design that needs a live network link for a critical decision has already failed the brief.
  • Our scope: one crop done deeply rather than many crops done shallowly.
The idea

Why a camera alone is not enough

A camera sees symptoms, not causes.

A yellowing paddy leaf looks the same whether the cause is nitrogen deficiency or water stress, and early Brown Spot can pass for either. No larger vision model fixes that, because the answer is not in the pixels. It is in the humidity history, the root-zone moisture and the soil chemistry. So KisanNetra keeps vision and sensors as separate components, joined by an explainable rule layer where every recommendation traces back to a measurement.

Corroboration ceiling

A confident camera reaches MEDIUM, never HIGH, on its own

HIGH risk also needs the sensors to agree; Blast, for example, needs humidity ≥ 85% at 24–32 °C. Tungro and Dead Heart are capped at MEDIUM by design, because their insect vectors are invisible to every sensor on this node, and the advisory says so.

Abstain

An unclear frame is a delayed capture, not a missed detection

Blurry frames are rejected before inference (sharpness score 2073 accepted; the same frame blurred, 9.6, rejected). Low-confidence pest calls are re-imaged. That costs little because the pole photographs the same plants every cycle.

No guessing

A failed sensor reads UNKNOWN, never zero

A cut soil-probe wire must not become "0 mg/kg nitrogen, buy fertiliser". Dependent advice is withheld and a manual check is requested. Automated tests prove a faulted probe cannot escalate an alert.

The system

One fixed pole, two processors, soil probes at two depths

The node does not move. Coverage grows by adding poles, which keeps the cost per plot low. An always-on microcontroller watches the sensors; the AI processor is powered off until there is something worth looking at.

Sentinel tier (always on, milliwatts)

ESP32 + LoRa
  • Reads soil moisture ×2, pH, EC, soil and air temperature, humidity, light, rain and tank level every 15 minutes
  • Keeps a rolling history, so it sees trends, not just snapshots
  • Wakes the AI tier when a stress threshold or drying trend is crossed, plus dawn and mid-morning captures
wake signal · sensor window over UART

Compute tier (off by default)

Qualcomm QCS6490
  • Captures a frame, applies the blur gate, then runs the disease and pest models (5.8 MB each)
  • Fuses the vision results with the sensor window and produces one advisory with its reasons
  • Queues results locally and syncs when a link exists, then powers down
advisory · display / SMS · optional cloud sync

Farmer

one trip per advisory
  • Disease and pest verdicts are kept separate but delivered as a single message, because the farmer walks to the field once.

Results

Two models, measured on images they never trained on

Both are MobileNetV3-Small, fine-tuned on Paddy Doctor field images from Tamil Nadu (ADT45, Ponni and other local varieties) plus a lab-condition rice set. Every headline figure carries a 95% Wilson confidence interval. Hover or focus a bar for its counts.

How we checked our own numbers. A separate script reloads each exported ONNX model and its class list and reproduces the accuracy with independently written preprocessing. 51 automated checks pass (23 fusion, 18 pest-fusion, 10 end-to-end). Our own audit found 15 issues in our work, including a label swap that reported healthy plants as Tungro; it was caught and fixed, and the audit is published in the repository.

Qualcomm edge path · future integration

Built for the QCS6490; Qualcomm integration is the next phase

Everything above was measured in software on a laptop CPU (FP32 ONNX, 2.5 ms per image; not a Qualcomm figure). The system is designed around the QCS6490 from the start, and bringing it onto Qualcomm silicon is planned in the phases below.

PhaseIntegration stepWhat it will prove
1Compile and profile both models on QCS6490 through Qualcomm AI HubLatency, peak memory, share of layers on the Hexagon NPU
2INT8 with Qualcomm's quantizer; quantization-aware training if neededINT8 accuracy against the FP32 baseline
3RB3 Gen 2 class board with the ESP32 sensor tier over UARTEnd-to-end on-device advisory and energy per capture session
  • Why this SoC: an NPU for the vision workload, a Linux application processor for fusion and camera support, at a power level the duty cycle makes solar-viable.
  • Why an ESP32 beside it: the QCS6490 exposes no user analog input, and capacitive soil probes are analog.
  • INT8: standard onnxruntime post-training quantization collapsed accuracy on both models (22–36%). The cause is not yet isolated, so FP32 ships; Qualcomm's own quantizer is part of the integration phase.

Limitations

What we have not shown yet

Stated here so you don't have to ask.

!

No physical board yet. Sensor values in every page are simulated by a generator with drift, noise and fault injection; the hardware is designed and modelled in CAD and in ESP32 firmware simulation.

!

Camera angle not yet tested. Training images are close-up leaves; the pole camera looks down at the canopy. The evaluation script is ready; field photographs at the mounting angle are the next thing we collect.

!

About 5% near-duplicate images between our training and test splits (measured by perceptual hashing), so headline accuracy is slightly optimistic. Re-reporting without them is under way.

!

Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium are estimates. Low-cost soil probes infer them from conductivity, so the system flags a possible shortage and recommends a soil test; it never prescribes fertiliser.

!

Coverage: five rice diseases and two pest-damage types today. Brown planthopper and leaf folder are next.

Roadmap

From validated software to a pole in a Thanjavur field

Qualcomm integration

Profile both models on QCS6490 through Qualcomm AI Hub; recover INT8 with Qualcomm's quantizer or quantization-aware training.

Next round

Build the physical node on an RB3 Gen 2 class board with the ESP32 sensor tier; collect canopy-angle images and measure accuracy on them.

Season pilot

One pole per zone on a cooperative's plots through a kuruvai or samba season; calibrate soil thresholds per field.

Scale

One compute node shared per cooperative with low-cost sensor nodes per plot; add brown planthopper and leaf folder detection.
